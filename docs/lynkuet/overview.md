# Lynkuet (elinzanetant): a dual NK1/NK3 antagonist joins the neurokinin class

**Approved** FDA, 24 October 2025 · **Sponsor** Bayer · **Dose** 120 mg (two 60 mg capsules) once daily at bedtime · **Indication** moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms due to menopause.

## What Lynkuet is

Lynkuet is elinzanetant, a non-hormonal small molecule that blocks the neurokinin-1 (NK1) and neurokinin-3 (NK3) receptors. The FDA approved it on 24 October 2025 for moderate-to-severe vasomotor symptoms (VMS) due to menopause. The label describes the mechanism as inhibition of substance P and neurokinin B signalling through NK1 and NK3 antagonism on kisspeptin/neurokinin B/dynorphin (KNDy) neurons, which can modulate thermoregulatory activity associated with hot flushes.

It is the first dual NK1/NK3 antagonist approved for this indication. Fezolinetant (Veozah), a selective NK3 antagonist from Astellas, has been on the US market since 12 May 2023. That pair is the class: one mechanism, two pharmacological strategies, two approvals.

## The class rests on one piece of basic science

KNDy neurons in the arcuate nucleus relay oestrogen signals to preoptic heat-dissipation pathways. Neurokinin B acting on NK3 is the main switch. Rance and colleagues set out the hypothesis in a 2013 review, building on 2012 ablation experiments showing that KNDy neurons facilitate cutaneous vasodilatation and participate in oestrogen modulation of body temperature. Fezolinetant tested the NK3 half of that hypothesis in the SKYLIGHT programme. Elinzanetant tests NK3 plus NK1; the dual mechanism is proposed to add sleep and mood benefit on top of VMS control, and OASIS-1/2 reported improvements in sleep disturbance and menopause-specific quality of life as secondary endpoints.

## The evidence

**OASIS-1 and OASIS-2 (JAMA 2024).** Two randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trials, 796 postmenopausal women aged 40-65, elinzanetant 120 mg once daily for 26 weeks versus placebo for 12 weeks followed by elinzanetant. At week 12, VMS frequency fell by 3.2 episodes per 24 hours more than placebo in both trials (OASIS-1: -3.2, 95% CI -4.8 to -1.6; OASIS-2: -3.2, 95% CI -4.6 to -1.9). Severity also improved significantly. The safety profile was favourable.

**OASIS-3 (JAMA Internal Medicine 2025).** A 52-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at 83 sites, 628 women. At week 12, VMS frequency fell by 5.4 episodes per day with elinzanetant versus 3.5 with placebo (least-squares mean difference -1.6, 95% CI -2.0 to -1.1; P<.001). The study was not powered for secondary or exploratory endpoints, which showed numerical advantages for sleep and quality of life. The authors report no hepatotoxic effects, endometrial hyperplasia, or meaningful bone-density changes; treatment-related adverse events were 30.4% versus 14.6%, mostly somnolence, fatigue and headache.

**OASIS-4 (NEJM 2025).** A phase 3 trial in 474 women with VMS caused by endocrine therapy for HR-positive breast cancer or its prevention. Elinzanetant reduced VMS frequency by 6.5 episodes per day at week 4 versus 3.0 with placebo, and by 7.8 versus 4.2 at week 12 (difference -3.4, 95% CI -4.2 to -2.5; P<0.001). Headache, fatigue and somnolence were the most common adverse events; serious adverse events were 2.5% versus 0.6% during weeks 1-12. This is the clinical expansion that takes the drug beyond the general menopause population.

**Fezolinetant's trials, for comparison.** SKYLIGHT 1 (Lancet 2023) randomised 527 women to placebo, fezolinetant 30 mg or 45 mg; at week 12, VMS frequency fell by 2.39 and 2.55 episodes more than placebo. SKYLIGHT 2 (JCEM 2023) found week-12 differences of -1.86 (30 mg) and -2.53 (45 mg). A 2023 network meta-analysis compared fezolinetant with hormone and non-hormone therapies, not with elinzanetant.

**Pooled safety (eClinicalMedicine 2026).** Four randomised, placebo-controlled studies (SWITCH-1 plus OASIS-1 to OASIS-3), 1519 participants at 12 weeks and 1113 elinzanetant-treated participants at 52 weeks. Treatment-emergent adverse events were 50.8% versus 43.2% over 12 weeks; exposure-adjusted incidence rates over 52 weeks were 196.61 versus 209.00 per 100 person-years. Headache, fatigue and somnolence were the most common events. There were no Hy's Law cases or cholestatic injury signal. Discontinuation was 7.8% versus 3.6%.

## Safety and monitoring

The two labels differ in how they treat the liver.

| | Lynkuet (elinzanetant) | Veozah (fezolinetant) |
|---|---|---|
| US approval | 24 Oct 2025 | 12 May 2023 |
| Mechanism | NK1 + NK3 antagonist | selective NK3 antagonist |
| Dose | 120 mg once daily at bedtime | 45 mg once daily |
| Boxed warning | none | hepatotoxicity (added Dec 2024) |
| Baseline liver tests | ALT, AST, ALP, total/direct bilirubin; do not start if ALT/AST or total bilirubin >=2x ULN | same |
| Follow-up liver tests | at 3 months | monthly for 3 months, then at 6 and 9 months |
| Nervous-system effects | somnolence, fatigue, vertigo, dizziness, presyncope in 11.9% vs 3.5% placebo; advise against driving until resolved | no comparable CNS warning |
| Renal | end-stage renal disease not recommended | severe renal impairment / ESRD contraindicated |
| Key interactions | avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers; reduce dose with moderate inhibitors | CYP1A2 inhibitors contraindicated |

Lynkuet is contraindicated in pregnancy because animal data suggest pregnancy loss or stillbirth; the label also warns about seizures in patients with a history of seizures. These are not class effects: they come from elinzanetant's own label, and fezolinetant's label has a different contraindication set.

## The literature gap

The class-internal evidence is thinner than the class story suggests.

1. **No head-to-head randomised trial.** The only comparative statement in the recent literature is indirect: a 2026 review says cross-study comparisons suggest a numerical sleep advantage for elinzanetant, but head-to-head trials are required to confirm it. An OpenAlex title/abstract search on 20 October 2026 found no head-to-head RCT.
2. **Elinzanetant's paper trail is young and concentrated in the OASIS programme.** OpenAlex indexes 178 works mentioning elinzanetant in title or abstract (16 in 2024, 51 in 2025, 102 in 2026 to date), against 297 for fezolinetant (38 in 2023, 75 in 2024, 82 in 2025, 69 in 2026 to date). Only 47 works mention both drugs. The OASIS programme is Bayer-sponsored, and the 2026 review is a two-author review, not a systematic review.
3. **The real-world literature is one-sided.** Both 2026 pharmacovigilance analyses are fezolinetant; they describe hepatic reporting signals in FAERS — 1282 reports with a median onset of 77 days in one, and 1757 cases with a median onset of 38 days in the other. There is no equivalent post-marketing literature for elinzanetant yet, which is expected for a drug approved in October 2025 but leaves the class comparison one-sided.
4. **OASIS-3 was not powered for its secondary endpoints.** The 52-week sleep and quality-of-life results are descriptive, not hypothesis tests. The same caution applies to the pooled safety analysis, which the authors label exploratory.
5. **Long-term hepatic and pregnancy data are still accumulating.** The Lynkuet label requires baseline liver tests and a repeat at 3 months, but not the monthly schedule fezolinetant carries. Whether that lighter schedule is adequate in real-world use is an open question; the fezolinetant pharmacovigilance papers argue for early monitoring, and the same logic has not yet been tested for elinzanetant.
6. **No class-level network meta-analysis including elinzanetant.** The 2023 network meta-analysis predates elinzanetant's approval and compares fezolinetant only with hormone and non-hormone therapies.

None of this undermines the approval. It defines what an honest overview can say: the efficacy story is well powered and consistent across OASIS-1 to OASIS-3 in the general menopause population, and replicated in the breast-cancer population in OASIS-4; the class comparison is not yet a randomized question.

The class map puts the neurokinin antagonist class at the centre. Two drug branches leave it: Veozah (fezolinetant) on the left, Lynkuet (elinzanetant) on the right. Each branch carries its sponsor, its mechanism node (selective NK3 versus dual NK1/NK3), and its trial reports.
